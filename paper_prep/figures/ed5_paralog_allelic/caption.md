**Extended Data Fig. 5 | Allelic, paralogous and community-level divergence in human subtelomeres.**

**a**, Cumulative distribution of substitution divergence between subtelomeric sequences, weighted by
aligned base pairs, for three classes: homologous copies of the same chromosome arm in different
individuals (*allelic*, blue, median 0.15%); copies on the same chromosome (*paralogous, same
chromosome*, orange, median 0.83%); and copies on different chromosomes (*paralogous, different
chromosome*, grey, median 0.89%). The two paralogous classes are indistinguishable from each other and
both lie 5.9-fold above the allelic class.

**b**, Each class median expressed as a fold-change over the allelic median, with the range occupied by
ancient duplicated gene families (100–1,000-fold) shown for scale. The in-community and
out-of-community bars are medians over arm pairs rather than over alignment records.

**c**, For each Leiden community, the median divergence between its member arms divided by the median
divergence between those arms and all non-member ends. Values below 1 indicate that members are more
similar to each other than to outside ends; 1.0 indicates no internal cohesion. Communities with fewer
than three internal arm pairs are omitted.

**d**, For each pair of chromosome arms, the per-record median divergence (magnitude) against the
fraction of aligned bases at ≥99.5% identity (tail). Pairs within a community (orange) are not shifted
to lower divergence but are enriched at high tail fractions.

**e**, For each of 45 chromosome ends, the divergence of its closest partner on a different chromosome,
measured as that partner's per-record median divergence. The allelic median (0.10%, dotted) and the
per-end median (0.52%, solid) are marked; 11 of 45 ends find a partner within twice the allelic
baseline.

Divergence is X/(=+X) from alignment CIGARs (substitutions only; indels excluded) in 2 kb windows over
the terminal 500 kb of each arm. Times, where quoted elsewhere, assume 1.2×10⁻⁸ substitutions per base
per generation and are upper bounds, because the subtelomeric and paternal-germline mutation rate is
higher than the genome average.
