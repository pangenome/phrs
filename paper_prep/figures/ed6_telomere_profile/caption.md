**Extended Data Fig. 6 | Paralogous-to-allelic divergence ratio as a function of distance from the
telomere.**

**a**, For each chromosome arm, the median divergence of that arm's sequences from non-homologous ends
divided by its allelic divergence, in 2 kb windows from the telomere–subtelomere junction inwards
(grey); black, median across 48 arms. The ratio rises from ~1.3 within the first 8 kb to ~6–7 beyond
300 kb.

**b**, The same quantity computed for each individual chromosome end (12,610 ends shown as grey
curves), with community medians overlaid: acrocentric short arms (C7) and PAR1 (C15) remain at 1.0
across the whole range, whereas the cohesive communities rise with distance.

**c**, Per-arm median ratio (windows ≥10 kb), grouped by community. Values at the dotted line (1.0)
indicate that non-homologous copies are as similar as alleles.

The hatched zone marks the terminal ~6 kb, which is not plotted: allelic divergence there is
0.3–2.5%, against 0.09–0.16% in the interior. Human intraspecific divergence is 0.1–0.3%, so the
identical-arm comparisons in that zone are not aligning homologous positions and the ratio has no
interpretation. Divergence is X/(=+X) from alignment CIGARs, substitutions only, in the terminal
500 kb of each arm; paralogous partners are restricted to other chromosomes.
