R2. "The chr5q/chr1p candidate recurs in two generations (PAN027 paternal and
    PAN028); it happens twice, so there must be some problem with it."
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS: OPEN — but the premise is wrong: the second occurrence is the
TRANSMITTED copy of the first, not an independent repeat. Evidence below.
(This is the Fig5 autosomal candidate the reviewer means — NOT chr9q/chr3q;
see R3 for that one. Fig5E also highlights a chr21p/chr4p run, whose chr4
partner is pericentromeric, not subtelomeric — lower priority.)

The two observations (sweepga f32 10:10 class-winner scan, whole-genome):

  G2: PAN027 paternal hap2 vs PAN011 — chr5q 182.052-182.080 Mb (28 kb),
      best inter-chr match PAN011 chr1 hap2, identity ~0.998 vs ~0.995 for
      the same-chr (chr5) match (narrow margin, ~0.003). Fig5B/C candidate.
      Flagger status: the 28 kb tract is flagger/problematic-NEGATIVE.
      (nearest flagger call: 182.112-182.144 Mb Col,COLLAPSE_OTHER — just
      distal of the tract)

  G3: PAN028 maternal hap1 vs PAN027 — chr5q 182.984-183.038 Mb (~54 kb
      chr1 h2 run, identities 0.9985-1.0 in f32 windows) plus a tail
      (183.09-183.11 Mb) that DOES fall inside a flagger Col,MISJOIN region
      (183.072-183.118583). The main 54 kb run is flagger-negative.
      Fig5E candidate.

What the sequence data show (wfmash v0.24.2-16-g4a35bfc5, -p 90, direct
test on the v1.1 polished assemblies, 2026-10-15):

  - PAN028's 56 kb chr5q region (182.984-183.040 Mb) maps to PAN027's
    PATERNAL chr5q (181.9-182.15 Mb window) in 4 split blocks at
    97.6-99.3% identity — including a 28.5 kb block at 182.047-182.076 Mb
    that overlaps the G2 tract interval — and has NO >=90% match to PAN027's
    paternal chr1p subtelomere (first 1.5 Mb), and none to the maternal
    chr5q copy either.
  => PAN028's maternal chr5q subtelomere descends from PAN027's PATERNAL
     chr5q — i.e. a meiotic crossover near the chr5q telomere in PAN027's
     germline put paternal subtelomeric sequence on the transmitted chr5,
     carrying the same standing chr1p-derived tract.

Therefore the recurrence is INHERITANCE, which is the expected signature of
a real transmitted structural feature — not a second independent event and
not a copy-paste artifact. The "it happens twice" argument inverts: an
assembly artifact tied to a shared repeat could also light up in both
scans, but the data show the G3 region is a ~97.7-99.3% match to the G2
tract's host copy on PAN027's paternal chr5q, i.e. one arrangement seen
in two meioses because it was transmitted.

Caveats that keep this honest (and are already in the manuscript):
  - wfmash -p95 does NOT reproduce the chr1p best-match for the G2 window
    (maps to chr5 only) — CROSS_ALIGNER_VALIDATION.md
    (fig5_donor_recipient_ribbon_draft). The manuscript already calls
    chr5q/chr1p the WEAKER, aligner-dependent candidate and chr9q/chr3q
    the better-supported one.
  - The f32 chr1p identities (0.998-1.0) exceed the wfmash chr5q-paternal
    identities (0.977-0.993) in the same region — consistent with drifted
    chr5q copies vs a fresher chr1p source, but needs the multi-way
    comparison to say anything precise.
