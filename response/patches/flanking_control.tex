%%% BEGIN INSERT (a): Results, replaces submission/paper.tex:291--296
The 100\,kb immediately centromere-ward of each PHR carries no shared
paralogous sequence and is uniquely mappable, and so provides the control for
this multi-mapping concern. Two statistics on the identical arm-pair set
(256 within-community and 2{,}670 between-community pairs in HG002) order the
two window types in opposite directions. The binary contrast $B/W$ (mean
between-community contact divided by mean within-community contact) is 0.0267
in PHR windows and 0.0021 in the flanks at 50\,kb, and 0.0280 against 0.0013 at
20\,kb; because a smaller $B/W$ means a stronger within-community contrast,
this metric places the unique-sequence flanks \emph{above} the PHR windows
(12.4- and 21.5-fold respectively). The arm-level Mantel $\rho$ between
sequence similarity and contact does the opposite: 0.657 for PHR windows
against 0.520 for the flanks in HG002, and 0.656 against 0.522 in CHM13
($p<10^{-4}$ in all four cases). The disagreement is a property of the two
statistics, not a contradiction in the data. $B/W$ is a ratio of two class
means, so it is carried by whichever term moves. Between PHR windows and flanks
the between-class mean contact changes by 6.7-fold (1.6$\times10^{-3}$ against
2.3$\times10^{-4}$ over the same 2{,}670 pairs) while the within-class mean
contact moves the other way by 1.85-fold (0.058 against 0.108); $B/W$ therefore
tracks the contact that crosses the community boundary, and at PHR coordinates
that boundary is crossed by sequence which arms in different communities still
share, the module repertoire overlapping at 0.57 between communities against
0.83 within them. It is not a measure of the contact the community partition
adds. A rank correlation over
the whole arm matrix is not a two-class statistic and is insensitive to a
background common to both classes; on it the PHR windows exceed the flanks in
both haplotypes at each of the three resolutions tested, and the gap widens as bins get
finer (10\,kb: 0.705 against 0.379 in HG002, 0.717 against 0.342 in CHM13).
Both statistics are biased in the same conservative direction by multi-mapping.
Placement among $k$ equally scoring loci is uniform, so a specific contact is
attenuated by roughly $1/k$ per end, and $1/k^2$ for a PHR--PHR contact; the
attenuation is symmetric across community classes, because a misplaced read is
as likely to land between communities as within one. Misplacement therefore
drives $\rho$ down and $B/W$ up, towards the null, and the uniquely mappable
flank windows carry essentially none of it. PHR-window enrichment is thus a
lower bound, and the bound is set by the biased measurement, which already
exceeds the unbiased flank value on the rank metric. A multi-mapping artefact
predicts the reverse ordering there, PHR below flank, which is not observed.

As a unique-sequence control for multi-mapping we repeated the calculation on
the 100\,kb immediately centromere-ward of each PHR, outside the called PHR and
over the same arm-pair set. We report both the $B/W$ contrast and the arm-level
Mantel $\rho$ for the control, because they answer different questions and, for
this comparison, rank the two window types oppositely: $B/W$ is a ratio of two
class means and is dominated by contact crossing the community boundary,
whereas $\rho$ is a rank correlation over all arm pairs and is invariant to a
background shared by both classes. In HG002 Hi-C at 50\,kb, $B/W$ is 0.0267 for
PHR windows and 0.0021 for the flanks while $\rho$ is 0.657 and 0.520; in CHM13
Hi-C the same comparison gives 0.0706 against 0.0567 and 0.656 against 0.522.
Because uniform placement of a multi-mapping read attenuates a specific contact
by about $1/k$ per end symmetrically across community classes, both PHR-window
statistics are biased towards the null, and the PHR-window enrichment they
report is a lower bound.
